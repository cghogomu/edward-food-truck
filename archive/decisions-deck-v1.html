Why Stripe for online,
Heartland for in-person.

Two systems is fine. Many small businesses run this way. Here's the breakdown:

Online — Stripe Checkout

Launch in 30 minutes

  • 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, published & flat
  • $0/month — no platform fee
  • Apple Pay + Google Pay built in (huge for ordering on a phone)
  • No phone calls, no rate negotiation, no rep
  • Quick to set up; widely trusted by small businesses
  • Automatic tax calculation available (I'll skip — see slide 11)
In-person — Heartland (keep)

Don't break what works

  • Your existing in-person card reader stays untouched
  • The rate you already have stays exactly the same
  • No retraining at the truck window
  • If Heartland later quotes a great online rate, adjusting or switching is fairly straightforward
The downside, named

You'd reconcile two systems. In practice: Stripe's dashboard handles online orders end-to-end. Heartland keeps handling the window. Reports are filed separately at tax time. This is a fair trade for launching weeks earlier.

How do you
find out an order came in?

Three paths, with pricing:

Option A

Email only

$0/mo
  • Resend free tier (3,000 emails/mo)
  • You get an instant order email
  • Customer gets receipt email with pickup time
  • You keep email visible during service — most truck owners already do
Option B

Email + SMS to you

~$3–5/mo
  • Resend (free) + Twilio for SMS to you only
  • Vibrate alert during service — hard to miss
  • ~$1/mo base + ~$0.008/text
  • Easy to add later (~1 hour) if A isn't enough
Option C

Full SMS for everyone

~$8–15/mo
  • You AND your customers get SMS
  • Twilio per-text fees scale with order volume
  • Nice but not expected for a food truck
  • Customer email confirmation is the norm
My pick

Launch with A. If emails get buried during rush, I can bolt on B in an afternoon. Don't pay for C until customers ask.

What do you charge
for delivery now?

You already deliver, so the simplest move is to keep your current fee. Tell me what it is and I'll plug it in. If you've been thinking about changing it, here are three common shapes for a flat-fee, 10-mile radius:

Friendly

$5

  • Encourages delivery, drives volume
  • May be slightly under cost on far-edge addresses
  • Best if you want delivery to take off
Balanced

$7

  • Covers gas, time, and truck wear comfortably
  • Sustainable margin for a 10-mile round trip
  • Middle of the road for self-delivery
Premium

$10

  • "I'll deliver but pickup is preferred"
  • Filters to higher-value orders
  • Possibly suppresses demand below break-even
Optional lever

Want a free-delivery threshold (e.g., free over $40)? Common upsell — encourages bigger orders and absorbs the fee in margin. Pick a threshold or skip for now.

Decision · 10 of 12

Sales tax —
fixed or auto?

Tax added at checkout, confirmed. Austin prepared food rate: 8.25% (6.25% TX state + 2% Austin/Travis County). Two ways to implement:

Recommended

Fixed at 8.25%

  • Free — no per-order fee
  • Calculated in the cart before checkout
  • Shown clearly on the receipt as its own line
  • Works perfectly for Austin-only delivery (which is your case)
  • If the rate ever changes, easy to update
Alternative

Stripe Tax

  • Auto-calculates based on customer address
  • $0.50 per order on top of the 2.9% + $0.30
  • Worth it only if you ever deliver outside Austin tax jurisdiction
  • Overkill for a 10-mile radius inside one city
Default

Going with the fixed rate unless you flag otherwise. Saves you $0.50/order forever — meaningful at 50+ orders/week.
